2. MEASUREMENT METHOD
---------------------

Recording:   the emulator ran with -qtrecord (PNG codec). Each recorded frame carries its emulated timestamp at
             the PC-FX field rate (59.8261 Hz), so durations are emulated time, independent of host speed.
             Intro runs were unthrottled. Runs with menu input ran at normal speed with key presses sent by a
             script (xdotool), timed so that the drive idle time before each load matches the capture.
Black gaps:  per-frame mean luma (ffmpeg signalstats). A frame is black below 31.3 on the capture (black level
             30.0) and below 17.5 on the emulator (black level 16.0). The two thresholds are the same level after
             the capture's luma scaling, measured on the title screen (capture 187.8, emulator 198.3) and on
             black. Fades step every 3 to 5 fields, so the threshold choice moves no edge by more than a field.
Intro:       Disc A, first frame of the NEC logo fade-in to the first frame of the title screen.
             Disc B Scenario intro, first video frame after the Scenario start load to the first frame of the
             wrestler select screen. Part 1 runs to the black hold in the middle, part 2 from the hold to the
             end of the video.
Drift:       31 scene cuts in the Disc A intro located on hardware and in the emulator, matched in order.
             "Max drift" is the largest hardware-minus-emulator time difference at any matched cut.
Pauses:      picture held for 5 or more fields during motion video, found by frame-to-frame difference.
             Hardware threshold 0.8 (capture noise floor), emulator threshold 0.05 (lossless recording).
             Counted in part 1 of the Disc B Scenario intro.
Clip change: entrance video change (first wrestler to second wrestler), found as a scene cut. Each change waits
             on one READ command, so it is a load measurement inside motion video.
Drive log:   a temporary test build logged every drive command, READ (start sector, head position, sector
             count, raw seek time), end of data and SCSI bus reset with emulated timestamps. The logging was
             removed from the final source. The final build was re-measured without it.
Fit:         emulated loads are serial (the game waits for each READ), so a change to the drive model moves a
             gap by the sum of the changed delays of the reads inside it. The drive parameters were fitted to the
             hardware gaps by linear least squares on that basis, then the final build was run and measured.
Pictures:    framemd5 of every recorded frame, with repeated frames collapsed and sequences compared in order.

Hardware results:

3. FINDINGS
-----------

3.1 Stock behaviour
  Disc A intro:     122.138 s, 2.078 s short of hardware. Max drift 1.750 s.
  Disc B Scenario:  45.531 s, 1.686 s short of hardware. Part 1: no pauses.
  Loads:            boot loads 3.6 s short, Disc A match load 5.4 s short (section 6).

3.2 Read pattern (drive log)
  Disc A intro:     one READ command of 14,847 sectors.
  Disc B Scenario:  one READ command of 4,241 sectors, played in about 45 s. The game uses about 63% of the
                    2x data rate on average.
  During playback the 64 KB drive FIFO fills and empties every 5 to 20 sectors (about every 0.1 s). The game
  takes data in bursts, and the drive stops whenever the FIFO is full.
  Loads:            the game resets the drive (SCSI bus reset, then TEST UNIT READY and MODE SELECT) before the
                    second and third boot reads and after the first read of each match load. The READ after a
                    reset follows it by 0.069 s in every case.

3.3 Cause of the short FMV
  On hardware, the drive stops reading when its buffer is full, restarts only after the game has drained the
  buffer to a low level, and the first data after a restart arrives later than it would from a drive that never
  stopped. When that wait outlasts the data the game still holds, the game holds the picture. These are the
  pauses visible in the capture. Mednafen's drive restarts the instant one sector fits in its 64 KB FIFO, with
  no delay, so the game never runs short.

3.4 Cause of the load differences
  Drive reset:  every load containing a reset runs 1.5 to 1.9 s long on hardware per reset. Mednafen's drive
                is ready at once after a reset.
  Spin-down:    the two loads that follow more than 10.5 s of drive idle run long beyond what the reset
                explains (Disc A title to menu after 13.4 s idle: +0.85 s; Disc A match load after 23.5 s idle:
                +3.8 s). Loads after 1.9 to 9.4 s idle show no such delay.
  Seek:         forward seeks are faster than backward seeks on hardware. One forward seek of 12,741 sectors
                plus its load takes 0.833 s on hardware, which leaves at most 0.27 s for the seek. The Scenario
                start load, with two backward seeks, needs about 0.85 s for those two seeks. One scale on the
                PC Engine curve leaves 0.32 s of error on that load (section 6.3).

3.5 Short black holds (not changed)
  The black holds before the Disc B title and before the Disc A intro contain no drive command. During each hold
  the game uploads graphics through the two VDC (HuC6270) data ports: about 60,000 writes to 0x404 and 0x504 in
  five fields before the Disc B title (temporary I/O probe, emulator). Mednafen charges 2 CPU clocks per VDC
  write. The hold length follows the upload time; the fades on both sides step at the same rate on hardware and
  in the emulator. With 36 extra clocks per data-port write, fitted on the Disc B title hold alone:
    Disc B NEC logo to title hold      hardware 0.400 s   stock timing 0.284 s   with the change 0.401 s
    Disc A NEC logo to intro hold      hardware 0.583 s   stock timing 0.485 s   with the change 0.602 s
    Disc A intro, NEC fade-in to title hardware 124.216 s stock timing 124.060 s with the change 124.176 s
  The same cost slows the VS-screen name dissolve before each match:
    VS-screen name dissolve            hardware 19 fields stock timing 18 fields with the change 34 fields
  A single per-write cost cannot match both, so the setting is kept (pcfx.vdc_data_write_clocks, section 4.9)
  but defaults to 0, stock timing. The holds stay about 0.1 s short.

3.6 What was ruled out
  CPU timing:           V810 flag-use stall on vs off changes the Disc A intro by 1 frame (0.017 s).
  ADPCM rate:           voice pitch matches hardware exactly.
  KRAM write cost:      +8 clocks per KRAM data-port write (gameblabla's DIAG7 figure) changes the Disc A intro by
                        0.016 s and the Disc B title hold by 1 field.
  CPU memory timing:    gameblabla's DIAG11 model (PCFXemu ef7b891: cache refill 7 clocks, DRAM page change
                        4 clocks, posted RAM writes, no uncached fetch penalty) changes the Disc B boot load by
                        0.016 s and the title hold by 0.
  Extra RAM wait:       +2 clocks on every RAM read, or +1 on every RAM write, changes the Disc B title hold by 0.
  Command latency:      a fixed delay per READ (gameblabla measured about 7 fields for TEST UNIT READY) fits as
                        -0.12 s per read, which is not physical. Left out.
  Read errors/retries:  the reference console and disc are in near-new condition.


4. CHANGES
----------

Each item lists what it is, where it is, the setting, the default and the stock-equivalent value.

4.1 Drive read-ahead model (new)
  What:     buffer size, restart level and restart delay for the PC-FX CD drive.
  Where:    cdrom/scsicd.cpp (SCSICD_Init, DoREADBase, RunCDRead, SCSICD_StateAction); settings in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Settings: pcfx.cd_buffer_kb         default 240    stock 64
            pcfx.cd_resume_level_kb   default 28     stock 0
            pcfx.cd_resume_ms         default 280    stock 0
  Source:   fitted to the Disc A and Disc B intros in the capture (section 5).
  Note:     the FIFO stores data in the next power-of-2 size and limits its fill to the set size. The save-state
            restore masks positions with the storage size, so any buffer size restores correctly. Save states
            made with a different pcfx.cd_buffer_kb do not restore the drive buffer.

4.2 Seek scales, forward and backward (existing setting redefined, one new)
  What:     multipliers on the PC Engine seek curve (get_pce_cd_seek_ms, seektime_pce.h, by Dave Shadoff),
            one for reads ahead of the head and one for reads behind it.
  Where:    cdrom/scsicd.cpp (DoREADBase); settings in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Settings: pcfx.cd_seek_scale        default 0.35   (forward; was one scale for both, previous value 0.6)
            pcfx.cd_seek_scale_back   default 0.69   (new)
            Stock Mednafen has no PC-FX seek delay.
  Source:   least-squares fit to 11 hardware load measurements (section 6).
  Config:   mednafen.cfg sets pcfx.cd_seek_scale, so the line must read 0.35 (or be deleted).

4.3 Drive reset delay (new)
  What:     after a SCSI bus reset the drive answers commands at once but delivers no data until this long after
            the reset. The power-on reset is covered by the same rule. The BIOS issues its first READ 4.9 s after
            power-on, so it sees no delay.
  Where:    cdrom/scsicd.cpp (VirtualReset, DoREADBase, SCSICD_StateAction); setting in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Setting:  pcfx.cd_reset_ms          default 1920   stock 0
  Source:   same fit (section 6). The fitted extra time at the READ 0.069 s after the reset is 1.854 s.
  Related:  gameblabla measured about 1 s of END OF VOLUME after a bus reset plus a spin-up on the first read
            (PCFXemu cb13e4e, DIAG5). His model returns error codes to the game. This one delays the data, which
            leaves the game's command flow unchanged.

4.4 Drive spin-down (new)
  What:     when the drive has delivered no sector for longer than pcfx.cd_spindown_s, the next READ pays a
            spin-up delay that grows with the idle time beyond that point, up to a limit.
  Where:    cdrom/scsicd.cpp (SCSICD_Power, DoREADBase, RunCDRead, CD-DA sector read, SCSICD_StateAction);
            settings in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Settings: pcfx.cd_spindown_s        default 10.5
            pcfx.cd_spinup_ms_per_s   default 300    stock 0
            pcfx.cd_spinup_max_ms     default 3900   (the delay at 23.5 s idle, the longest idle in the capture)
  Source:   same fit (section 6).
  Note:     the idle and reset timers are not saved in save states. After a state load the drive counts as
            spinning with no reset pending.
  Related:  gameblabla's model applies a 0.6 s spin-up after 1.5 s idle (cb13e4e). The capture shows no delay
            after 9.4 s idle (Disc B match load), so that threshold is not used.

4.5 Sector delivery scale (new, left at 1.0)
  What:     multiplier on the per-sector delivery time.
  Where:    cdrom/scsicd.cpp (RunCDRead); setting in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Setting:  pcfx.cd_sector_scale      default 1.0    stock 1.0
  Source:   an early fit used 1.077 (Disc A 124.127 s, max drift 0.579 s, Disc B unchanged at 45.666 s).
            The drive read-ahead model replaces it and fits both discs at 1.0.

4.6 KRAM data-port write cost (new, left at 0)
  What:     extra CPU clocks per write to KING register 0x0E through port 0x604 or the 0xBC000000 window.
  Where:    pcfx/king.cpp, king.h, io-handler.inc, mem-handler.inc; setting in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Setting:  pcfx.kram_write_clocks    default 0      stock 0
  Source:   gameblabla, PCFXemu, DIAG7 measurement (12.2 clocks per dense OUT.H against about 4 in stock).
            No measurable effect on Queen of Queens timing (section 3.6).

4.7 V810 flag-use pipeline stall (ported)
  What:     2 extra clocks when a conditional branch, SETF, or STSR PSW immediately follows an instruction
            that writes the PSW condition flags. Unconditional BR is exempt.
  Where:    hw_cpu/v810/v810_cpu.cpp, v810_cpu.h, v810_oploop.inc; setting in pcfx/pcfx.cpp. PC-FX only.
            The Virtual Boy core keeps stock timing.
  Setting:  pcfx.v810_flag_stall      default 1      stock 0
  Source:   hardware measurement by David Shadoff (FPGA work, 2025), as cited in gameblabla's PCFXemu commit
            cc3ab0b, which also supplies the implementation and opcode table.
  Verified: cycle test on this core, fast and accurate modes:
              add -1,r18 / bne loop                      4 clocks stock, 6 clocks with stall (hardware: 6)
              add -1,r18 / nop / bne loop                5 clocks both (no adjacent flag writer)
              add / setf / mov / cmp / bne loop          7 clocks stock, 11 with stall (two stalls)

4.8 Timer zero flag (ported)
  What:     the timer's zero flag (control D2) sets on every underflow even with the timer interrupt (D0)
            disabled. The interrupt line asserts only when D0 and D2 are both set.
  Where:    pcfx/timer.cpp (FXTIMER_Update, FXTIMER_Write16).
  Source:   measured by gameblabla on a real PC-FX (PCFXemu commit 5b28a36, DIAG9 K14).

4.9 VDC data-port write cost (new, left at 0)
  What:     extra CPU clocks for each write to a VDC data port (0x404 VDC-A, 0x504 VDC-B), on top of the stock
            2 clocks. Register-select writes keep the stock cost.
  Where:    pcfx/io-handler.inc (port_wbyte, port_whword); setting in pcfx/pcfx.cpp.
  Setting:  pcfx.vdc_data_write_clocks   default 0    stock 0
  Source:   36 fits the Disc B NEC logo to title hold and the Disc A intro, but nearly doubles the VS-screen name
            dissolve (section 3.5), so the default is stock timing.

Reviewed in PCFXemu and not ported (repository rechecked November 3, 2026; last commit ef7b891, November 1):
  KING SCSI interrupt latch change (5b28a36): affects every CD read and replaces long-standing Mednafen code.
  VDC pre-mix change (2c9166a): contradicts HuC6261 manual section 2.3.2 (upper VDC selected on chroma key).
  Measured seek curve (seektime_pcfx.c, cb13e4e): forward 95 to 100 fields beyond 4,000 sectors at 90% scale,
    about 1.5 s for a 12,741-sector forward seek. The capture leaves at most 0.27 s for that seek (section 3.4).
    The curve also makes backward seeks faster than forward ones. The capture shows the reverse.
  Command latency, 1.5 s spin-down threshold, post-reset error codes (cb13e4e): see sections 3.6 and 4.3 to 4.4.
  CPU memory timing (ef7b891): no effect on Queen of Queens (section 3.6).
  K-BUS grant model, strict CD drive model, Mode 2 Form 2 video, video presentation filters: large rewrites in
    a different code base, marked tentative, or for homebrew discs and display.

8. GAMEPLAY LATENCY BETWEEN TECHNIQUES
--------------------------------------

Measured: the match-screen gap before each move video. A move video is detected when the screen's side strips
go black around the centre window. Both hardware matches and both emulator matches are COM VS COM, started with
RUN on the VS screen about as long after the VS screen appears as on hardware (Disc A 6.8 s, Disc B 3.5 s).

  Match                                   Moves   Gap min   10th pct   Median   Fastest gaps
  --------------------------------------  ------  --------  ---------  -------  -------------------------
  Hardware, Disc A (Aja vs Hasegawa)      23      0.833 s   1.090 s    2.583 s  0.83, 0.90, 1.07, 1.18
  Final build, Disc A                     26      1.203 s   1.404 s    2.833 s  1.20, 1.30, 1.40, 1.40
  Hardware, Disc B (Aja vs Sion)          29      0.917 s   1.050 s    1.933 s  0.92, 0.93, 1.05, 1.05
  Final build, Disc B                     27      0.384 s   1.250 s    2.541 s  0.38, 1.09, 1.17, 1.30

Limits: the computer opponents choose different moves on hardware and in the emulator from the first move on,
so the matches are not move-for-move comparisons. Most gaps include the computer's decision time. The 0.384 s
emulator gap is a two-part move (Aja climbs, then the elbow drop video) with one read between the parts.

Long-seek check (Disc B match, final build settings):
  Method:  each hardware move video was matched by picture to the same video in the emulator match (correlation
           0.92 to 0.97 for 19 of 28 transitions). The emulator drive log gives each video's disc position, so the
           seek between consecutive hardware videos is known. Aja's move videos sit near sectors 40,000 to 78,000,
           Sion's near 241,000 to 324,000, so a change of attacker is a seek of about 275,000 sectors.
  Result:  time left for the computer's decision = hardware gap - (modelled seek + data and CPU time).
             Backward 283,246 sectors (seek 1.40 s):  0.66 s left
             Forward 275,723 sectors (seek 0.72 s):   0.48 s left
             Forward 283,095 sectors (seek 0.74 s):   1.16 s left
           Apart from one two-part move (0.02 s) and one 0.14 s case, the emulator match's shortest decisions
           are 0.67 to 0.72 s. The backward long seek agrees within about 0.05 s. The forward long seek is at most about 0.2 s long; one sample.
  Tools:   vmatch.py, seekcheck.py, seekpce.py (section 10).


9. OPEN ITEMS
-------------

9.1 Black holds
  What:    three black holds run short in the emulator with no drive command inside them.
             Disc B NEC logo to title          hardware 0.400 s (24 fields), final build 0.284 s (17 fields)
             Disc A NEC logo to intro          hardware 0.583 s, final build 0.485 s (contains one seek)
             Disc B Scenario intro mid hold    hardware 1.017 s, final build 0.869 s
  Cause:   the first two follow the VDC upload time (section 3.5). pcfx.vdc_data_write_clocks 36 corrects them
           but slows the VS-screen name dissolve, so it is off by default. The Scenario mid hold has no VDC
           uploads (I/O probe) and sits inside one streaming READ; its cause is not identified.
  Effect:  the Disc A intro measurement and the Disc B Scenario total each include one of these holds.

9.2 Spin-up curve
  What:    the spin-up rate and threshold rest on two loads (section 6.3). Idle times between 13.4 s and 23.5 s
           are interpolated and longer idle times are held at the 3.9 s limit. Matches do not leave the drive
           idle long enough to reach the threshold.
